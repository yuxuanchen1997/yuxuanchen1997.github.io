---
title: "The Café That Misplaced Tuesday"
date: 2026-09-07 00:00:00
lang: en
layout: post
---

At six each morning, Mara unlocked the café and found a new door drawn in chalk behind the espresso machine. The first was no larger than a saucer. The next reached her knees. By Thursday, though Thursday had begun calling itself Saturday, the chalk door had grown tall enough to admit a customer.

Mara was a barista by employment and an interpreter of impossible weather by habit. She understood foam that formed maps, spoons that pointed toward arguments, and rain that fell upward whenever someone lied about being happy. Still, she did not open the door. She wrote TODAY’S SPECIAL across it and served cardamom coffee until noon.

Then the city misplaced Tuesday.

Clocks stepped from Monday directly into Wednesday. Bakeries discovered yesterday’s bread still dreaming in their ovens. Children searched their pockets for a school day they distinctly remembered spending. Only Mara’s café retained a thin flavor of Tuesday: warm dust, unfinished sentences, and the blue taste of anticipation.

A nimble little creature emerged from the chalk door. It had elbows like folded paper and six bright shoes, each tapping a different rhythm. Without asking, it sprang across cups, balanced on the grinder, and stole the word Tuesday from Mara’s menu.

“Put that back,” Mara said.

The creature swallowed the word. Immediately, its middle shoes began ringing like bicycle bells.

Mara pulled two shots of espresso into the smallest cup. She dusted the surface with chalk scraped from the door, then drew a clock in the foam. Its hands pointed between moments.

The creature leaned closer. Mara snapped her towel through the air, nimble in her own way, and caught all six shoes at once. The stolen day tumbled from its mouth, crumpled and smelling of cinnamon.

She ironed Tuesday flat beneath the hot milk pitcher. Outside, traffic paused as calendars shivered. Somewhere, a dentist remembered an appointment; somewhere else, a pear resumed falling from a table.

Mara pinned Tuesday between Monday and Wednesday on the wall. The creature, now barefoot and embarrassed, slipped back through the chalk.

By evening, the door had vanished. In its place stood one pale sentence: TOMORROW OWES YOU NOTHING.

Mara considered this, wiped away the final word, and left the rest for breakfast.
