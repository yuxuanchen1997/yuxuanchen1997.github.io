title: "The Waterfall Tailor"

Every morning, the waterfall descended behind Mara’s kitchen, though no mountain stood above it. Water simply unfolded from a blue seam in the air, struck the cabbage patch, and climbed away underground. Its voice filled the cups in the cupboard. If Mara lifted one to her ear, she heard teaspoons swimming upstream.

One Tuesday, a yellow cat stepped out of the falling water. It was perfectly dry and wore a measuring tape around its neck. Without greeting her, it inspected the kitchen chairs, the stove, and Mara’s elbows. Then it pointed one white paw at the waterfall and said, “It has become too large for the weather.”

Mara followed the cat through the cabbage patch. Up close, the waterfall shivered like a curtain trying to remember a window. The cat measured its width, its roar, and the distance between two drops. “We must make it snug,” it said. “Otherwise Thursday will slip through.” Mara did not know what Thursday looked like, but she had always liked its dependable position in the week.

They worked until noon. Mara hemmed the edges with green thread pulled from bean vines. The cat sat beneath the torrent, pedaling a small black sewing machine that had appeared where a pumpkin had been. Each stitch tightened the water. Fish-shaped buttons surfaced and fastened themselves. The waterfall narrowed from a shining wall to a silver coat, then to a sleeve, then to something no wider than a scarf.

At last, the cat wrapped the waterfall around Mara’s shoulders. It was cool but strangely snug, murmuring against her neck. Beyond the garden, the sky gave a relieved sigh, and Thursday—transparent, carrying a basket of unopened hours—walked safely past.

The cat packed its machine into the hollow of a cabbage. “Keep the collar buttoned when it rains,” it advised, and vanished down the kitchen drain.

Mara wore the waterfall all winter. Whenever the days grew loose and threatened to fall apart, she drew it closer. Inside its folds, teaspoons continued their silver migration, and sometimes a yellow paw stirred the current, measuring the distance to spring.
