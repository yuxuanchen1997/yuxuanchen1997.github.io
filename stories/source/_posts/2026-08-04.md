title: "The Pineapple Tide"

Every morning at six, the sea delivered a pineapple to the doorstep of Sailor Ansel’s house. It arrived upright, its green crown neatly combed, a collar of foam around its waist. Ansel had not sailed for eleven years, yet he still wore his blue coat daily and polished its brass buttons until each held a tiny, curved horizon.

At first he ate the pineapples. Their flesh tasted of salt, bells, and rooms he had forgotten. Soon there were too many. He built chairs from their skins, roofed his kitchen with their leaves, and rolled the unopened fruit into the spare room, where they murmured all night like passengers discussing an uncertain destination.

On the thirty-third morning, no pineapple appeared. Instead, the sea itself waited outside, folded into a small blue suitcase. A paper label tied to its handle read: RETURN TO SHIP.

Ansel carried the suitcase through town. With every step, fish-shaped shadows swam beneath the pavement. The baker’s loaves began rocking in their baskets. The clockmaker’s clocks filled with clear water, though they continued ticking. Behind Ansel rolled the pineapples, hundreds of them, solemn and fragrant, forming a daily procession that shopkeepers accepted as calmly as weather.

At the harbor there were no boats, only a vast wooden table set with white plates. Ansel placed the suitcase at its center. It sprang open, and the sea unfolded across the table, spilling neither left nor right but upward. Waves climbed the air. Blue distances hung over the plates. At the highest crest floated Ansel’s old ship, its sails patched with grocery lists and unfinished letters.

The pineapples boarded first. They lined the rails, their crowns trembling in the wind. Ansel followed and found no crew, only thirty-three empty chairs facing the stern. He understood without understanding: the voyage required a sailor, but the destination required witnesses.

At six the next morning, the town awoke beside an endless orchard. Pineapple plants stretched to every horizon, their roots drinking seawater from invisible tides. Far above, a ship sailed through the blue branches. Each day it dropped one polished brass button. Each day someone found it on a doorstep, warm and shining, with a tiny sailor waving from inside.
