At nine each morning, the rain reported for work. It entered through the revolving doors in narrow gray suits, shook itself over the tiles, and queued beneath the clock. By noon, every corridor smelled of wet paper. Nobody complained. The Bureau of Unfinished Weather had been built for such employees.

Mara worked at a desk that grew slightly whenever it rained. On dry days it was modest, with three drawers and a green lamp. During long storms, it stretched from wall to wall, sprouting cupboards, pigeonholes, and polished wooden peninsulas. Mara spent her hours walking around it, checking forms that requested fog for orchards, wind for laundries, and small private clouds for people who disliked being observed.

One especially rainy Tuesday, she found a check beneath her blotter. It was made payable to BEARER, signed by the rain, and dated tomorrow. The amount, written in blue ink, was ONE CLEAR AFTERNOON. On the back were two boxes: DEPOSIT and RETURN. Mara checked neither. She held the paper to her ear and heard children drawing hopscotch squares on a sunlit pavement.

Her supervisor examined it through a glass of water. “A clerical temptation,” he said. “If you cash it, somewhere else must pay.” He pointed toward the windows, where umbrellas drifted past without people beneath them. “Weather keeps strict accounts.”

All afternoon the desk continued growing. It pushed through a partition, nudged the elevator aside, and unfolded a narrow bridge toward the opposite building. From its newest drawer came a dry, warm breeze smelling of chalk. Mara imagined the promised afternoon arriving alone, with no morning before it and no evening afterward, waiting forever for someone to step inside.

She took her pen and drew a third box on the check: SHARE. The moment she marked it, the desk shuddered and became ordinary again. Outside, the rain thinned into bright threads. Each person in the city received exactly one minute of clear sky, though not at the same time.

Mara’s minute came at closing. Sunlight opened across her desk like an envelope. Inside lay another check, still damp, made payable to the rain.
