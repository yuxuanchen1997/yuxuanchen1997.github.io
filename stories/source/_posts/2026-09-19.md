title: "The Unnamed Tile"

In the city of Pale Steps, every shop had a floor that remembered another place. The baker’s tiles remembered wheat fields; the tailor’s, a windy station. Nera, the florist, inherited twelve white tiles that remembered nothing. However many stems she arranged above them, they remained blank and warm, like plates awaiting an impossible meal.

One rainy afternoon, a child traded a blue marker for rosemary. “It writes only where words are missing,” the child said. After closing, Nera uncapped it. The ink smelled of crushed leaves. On the nearest tile she wrote TULIP. A red cup pushed through the glaze, opening soundlessly, its stem descending as if the floor contained a garden.

She wrote LILY, FERN, and PEAR BLOSSOM on three more tiles. By morning, customers crowded the shop, watching roots swim beneath the ceramic surface. Yet whenever Nera cut a flower, a street outside changed its name. Tulip Lane appeared beside the post office; Fern Alley curled through the market. Nobody objected. They simply adjusted their errands.

The blank tiles, Nera understood, had not forgotten a place. They were waiting to invent one. She drew arrows between the names, and the city obeyed. Bridges turned toward balconies. A staircase reached the river and continued beneath it, perfectly dry. People brought requests on seed packets: a shortcut to school, a corner where soup never cooled, a bench facing yesterday.

Then an inspector came carrying a ruler as long as a broom. He declared the city improperly arranged and ordered Nera to erase the floor. The marker dragged backward, swallowing blue lines, while outside streets snapped straight. The underwater staircase rose dripping into traffic. Tulip Lane folded shut with three houses inside, their residents calmly waving from the windows.

Nera stopped. On the last white tile, she drew no flower and wrote no destination. She placed a dot. From it grew a small green door, hinged on a stem. The inspector measured it, frowned, and stepped through. His ruler followed, bending politely.

The door closed. Across the city, every displaced road relaxed into a curve. Nera capped the marker and set it in a vase. By evening it had grown roots. She left the final tile unnamed. Sometimes, while arranging bouquets, she heard footsteps beneath it and the careful click of someone measuring a country that had not yet decided its shape.
