title: "The Shape Beyond the Gate"
date: 2026-08-10 00:00:00

Every morning, the gateway appeared in a different wall of Mara’s apartment. On Monday it occupied the kitchen tiles, outlined in blue grout. On Tuesday it interrupted a bookshelf, slicing several dictionaries neatly in half. By Thursday it had shrunk to the size of a postage stamp beside the bathtub tap.

Mara never entered. She had learned that caution was a kind of furniture: plain, useful, and always placed between the body and the unknown.

On Friday, an envelope slid beneath the gateway, although the gateway had chosen the ceiling. The envelope drifted downward like a leaf reconsidering autumn. It was addressed in Mara’s handwriting to “The Shape I Used to Be.”

Inside lay a paper silhouette. At first it resembled a key, then a spoon, then a small animal sleeping with its nose beneath its tail. Whenever Mara blinked, its shape changed. She placed it on the table, but the table softened around it, becoming briefly a pond, then a map of a city whose streets all ended at her chair.

The envelope whispered. Not words exactly, but the dry sound of someone turning pages in another room.

Mara carried both envelope and shape to the gateway. Today it stood in the hallway, tall enough for her, filled not with darkness but with a yellow afternoon. Beyond it, hundreds of people walked backward through a market, returning fruit to trees and flour to patient sacks. A violin assembled itself inside an open case. Rain rose from umbrellas into a cloud shaped like an empty hand.

The paper silhouette became Mara’s profile.

She understood then—not clearly, but with the certainty of recognizing a forgotten scent—that the gateway did not lead elsewhere. It led to everything she had ceased becoming.

She sealed the silhouette inside the envelope and pushed it through. The backward market stopped. Every face turned toward her, including faces she might once have worn.

Then the envelope returned, heavier. Inside was a brass doorknob, warm from another palm.

Mara fixed it to the air beside the gateway and turned it. Her apartment opened like a folded letter. Rooms spread into streets; cupboards filled with weather; the ceiling lifted its white lid.

For the first time, the gateway remained where it was.

Mara stepped through neither entrance. Smiling, she chose the new door she had made in the air.
