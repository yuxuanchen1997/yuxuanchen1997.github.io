Every Tuesday, the moon came down to buy buttons from Miriam's basement shop. It arrived wearing a raincoat, though the sky was dry, and spoke through a mouthful of moths. Miriam never asked why a moon needed buttons. She simply opened the cedar drawers and let it choose.

This Tuesday, the moon selected thirteen black buttons and paid with a spoon still warm from someone's dream. After it left, Miriam noticed the buttons had not gone. Instead, thirteen tiny moons sat in the tray, humming the same note. They rolled toward her sewing machine and arranged themselves into the shape of an address.

Miriam copied it onto her palm: 7 Fishbone Lane, Behind Yesterday.

She locked the shop, stepped through the pantry, and found herself on a street paved with sleeping photographs. Each house blinked when she passed. At number seven, a brass mailbox coughed politely and spat out a velvet envelope. Inside was an invitation to her own childhood, scheduled for midnight.

At midnight, Miriam entered the house and found a dinner table set for everyone she had almost become: sailor, dentist, thief, lighthouse, and queen of beetles. They were arguing over soup that whispered secrets backward. At the head of the table sat a small girl with Miriam's eyes and a crown made of unpaid bills.

"You forgot me," the girl said.

"I was busy surviving," Miriam answered.

The girl nodded, then handed her a button shaped like a door. Miriam sewed it onto her coat. The room unbuttoned around her. Dawn spilled in, smelling of salt and burnt sugar.

Back in the basement shop, the moon waited, bare and pale without its coat. "Well?" it asked.

Miriam smiled. "I'll need thread."

The moon opened its raincoat. Inside, galaxies tangled like yarn. Something inside her coat began singing.
