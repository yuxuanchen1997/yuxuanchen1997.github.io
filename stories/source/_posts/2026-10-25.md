---
title: "The Half-Turn"
date: 2026-10-25 00:00:00
lang: en
layout: post
---

At noon, the city folded its streets into fans, and I could no longer find the bakery. In its place stood a narrow blue door planted upright in the pavement, unsupported by any wall. Its brass knob was warm and slowly turning by itself, as if an invisible hand were considering whether to invite me in.

I turned it first. Beyond the door was a room made entirely of crystal, including the air, which chimed whenever I breathed. In the center, a peony grew from a teacup. Its petals were closed around something that ticked. A small card lay beside it: TURN ONLY WHEN THE FLOWER ASKS.

I waited. The peony said nothing, but the knob appeared among its leaves, though I had left it on the door. When I touched it, every crystal surface clouded with scenes from afternoons I had forgotten: a red kite snagged on a chimney, my father peeling an orange in one unbroken ribbon, a train carrying only empty chairs toward the sea.

The ticking quickened. One petal loosened, then another. Inside the blossom sat a tiny blue door, exact in every detail. Its knob waited beneath my finger. I understood, without knowing why, that turning it would return the bakery, the ordinary streets, and the loaf I had promised to bring home. Not turning it would let the room continue remembering for me.

The peony whispered—not in words, but by releasing the scent of rain on hot stone. I turned the knob halfway.

The crystal room cracked into a thousand clear corridors. In each, another version of me stood before another flower, hand paused at the same angle. Some smiled. Some wept without sadness. One had no face, only a bright window full of bread.

Then the peony opened completely. The ticking stopped, and all the corridors leaned toward me like stalks seeking light. I released the knob.

When I stepped back onto the pavement, the bakery was there. The loaf under my arm was warm. Yet whenever I reached a door, any door, its knob turned halfway before my hand arrived, and somewhere inside the walls a peony began counting the afternoons I had not yet lived.
