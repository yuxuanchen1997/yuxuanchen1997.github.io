---
title: "The Footpath’s Dream"
date: 2026-10-29 00:00:00
lang: en
layout: post
---

Every afternoon, Mara walked the footpath behind the abandoned weather station, though no map admitted it existed. The path was pale as flour and changed its curves whenever she blinked. On dreamy days, when the hills seemed stuffed with feathers, it led her past blue thistles and returned her home before she had taken ten steps.

One evening, a fox sat across it, holding a brass key between its teeth. Its coat was not red but the orange of a half-remembered song. When Mara approached, the fox placed the key on the ground and spoke without moving its mouth: “The footpath has misplaced its dream. Will you help me carry it back?”

At once, the footpath rolled itself up like a ribbon, revealing a narrow staircase descending into warm soil. Mara followed the fox down. Beneath the hill stood an orchard of doors, each growing from a silver trunk. Behind one, rain was knitting a sweater. Behind another, spoons rehearsed the names of rivers. The fox ignored them all.

At the last door, the brass key began to hum. It opened a room no larger than a cupboard. Inside floated the missing dream: a small glass bridge crowded with sleeping shoes. Each shoe whispered a destination—bakery, lighthouse, childhood, Tuesday—but none remembered how to arrive. The bridge trembled, making a sound like cups in a distant kitchen.

Mara understood that the footpath had grown tired of always knowing where it went. She stepped onto the glass bridge and turned her own shoes backward. The fox followed, sweeping the whispered destinations into its tail. Together they crossed without choosing a direction. With every step, the bridge softened, becoming dust, then moss, then the familiar pale path under an evening sky the color of pear skin.

By the weather station, the fox returned the key to its mouth. The path lay quiet, but it no longer hurried Mara home. It wandered around stones, paused to circle a puddle twice, and occasionally climbed a tree for no reason. Mara kept walking. Behind her, the fox erased their footprints with its bright, impossible tail.
