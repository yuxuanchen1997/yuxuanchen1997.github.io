title: "The Far Side of Tuesday"

Every morning, Liora arrived at the hotel before its doors remembered they were doors. The lobby was misty, though no weather entered; pale vapor rose from the carpet and collected beneath the chandeliers like unfinished ceilings. At the center stood an oak tree where the reception desk should have been. Its trunk contained a brass bell, a ledger, and a narrow drawer that smelled of pencils.

As the hotel’s only receptionist, Liora had learned to read the oak’s rings. Each ring was a floor, each knot a room, and each acorn that dropped into the mist announced a guest. Most acorns carried ordinary names. On Tuesday, however, one landed on the bell and split open. Inside was a tiny key tagged LOBBY. At once, the walls took one quiet step backward. The entrance vanished. Beyond the windows, delivery bicycles circled without finding the building.

A guest in a yellow raincoat emerged from the vapor, dragging a suitcase that appeared to be full of soft applause. “I reserved the lobby,” they said.

“People pass through the lobby,” Liora replied. “They do not stay in it.”

The guest opened the suitcase. Applause escaped, pattering among the leaves. The oak flushed green from roots to rafters, and the mist began forming furniture: translucent chairs, a dripping piano, a staircase that climbed into itself. In the ledger, every reservation changed to the same word: HERE.

Liora understood that if she handed over the key, the lobby would become a room, and a room must have walls. Everyone already inside—the guests, the staff, perhaps even Wednesday—would be shut away from arrival. Yet refusing a valid reservation was forbidden. The rule was carved into the oak beneath twelve layers of varnish.

She rang the brass bell twice and addressed the tree. “Are you checking in, or checking out?”

The oak considered this. Sap moved beneath its bark like an elevator. Then a new ring widened around the trunk, carrying desk, bell, and ledger outward until Liora found herself standing on the far side of Tuesday. The missing entrance appeared between two branches.

She gave the tiny key to the guest. They unlocked a patch of mist and stepped inside, suitcase first. The applause softened to the sound of distant rain.
