title: "The Spine of the Marsh"

Every spring, the marsh misplaced its horizon. Reeds leaned into the sky, clouds floated under the water, and travelers walked in circles until their pockets filled with tadpoles. Only Mara, the village bookbinder, could tell one direction from another. She carried a needle threaded with red silk and stitched each morning to the next.

One April, the marsh began shedding pages. They rose from the mud blank and damp, flapping among the herons. Mara gathered them in baskets, pressed them beneath stones, and waited for words to appear. Instead, tiny doorways formed in the paper. Behind each doorway stood the same porcupine, wearing spectacles made from two drops of rain.

“Your world has come unbound,” he said. “I am the index.”

He climbed through the largest page and shook himself. Several quills fell loose. Wherever a quill landed, the marsh forgot something: frogs forgot how to jump, water forgot how to reflect, and Mara forgot the sound of her own name.

She chased the porcupine across islands of trembling grass. He was not fleeing, exactly; he paused often to alphabetize pebbles or correct the spelling of wind. At last he stopped beside a pool shaped like an open hand.

“The marsh wants covers,” he explained. “Without them, everything leaks into everything.”

Mara looked around. A fish was swimming through a willow. Three clouds had nested in her basket. Her red thread stretched behind her, binding miles of mornings together.

She cut it.

At once, yesterday drifted away like a loose sleeve. Tomorrow rolled into the reeds and hid. With the freed thread, Mara sewed the first blank page to the eastern bank and the last to the west. The porcupine offered his remaining quills as needles. Together they bound mud, rain, roots, and wandering paths between covers woven from rushes.

By evening, the marsh had become a book too large to lift. Its pages turned whenever the wind changed its mind. Mara could no longer remember her name, but on the spine, embossed in gold pollen, appeared a single word.

She read it aloud. Every frog jumped. Every pool returned a face. The horizon clicked neatly into place, and the porcupine closed the day with both paws.
