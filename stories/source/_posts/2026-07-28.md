title: "The Orchard of Borrowed Ages"

At the edge of Bellweather stood an orchard where no fruit grew twice. One morning the branches bore brass peaches, warm as teacups, each stamped with a different age: seven, nineteen, forty-three, one hundred and two.

Mara, the town engraver, was summoned to correct them. She arrived with her burins in a velvet roll and found the orchard keeper dancing between the trunks. He was a jovial man named Pell, whose laughter shook screws from the peaches and sent them skipping through the grass.

“The numbers are leaking,” he explained cheerfully.

Indeed, a silver 8 had crawled onto his sleeve. A 3 slept beneath his hat. Wherever a number escaped, its peach became youthful—first smooth and shining, then small and green, then a blossom tightly folded around an invisible bee.

Mara climbed a ladder and began engraving the ages more deeply. But each cut opened a tiny window. Through one she saw herself at twelve, teaching spoons to swim in a rain barrel. Through another, she saw Pell as an old man, still laughing, though his laughter emerged as bright yellow ribbons that tied the trees together.

“Perhaps,” Mara said, “an age is not a label but a door.”

Pell considered this and bit into a peach marked thirty. At once he became thirty versions of himself, all jovial, all arguing about the weather. Some wore boots; one carried a violin full of salt; another had leaves instead of eyebrows. They surrounded Mara with helpful advice until she struck the peach with the flat of her tool.

The thirty Pells folded neatly back into one.

By afternoon, Mara had a new plan. On every peach she engraved not a number but an action: BEGIN, WANDER, LISTEN, RETURN. The orchard shivered. Ages flew from the trees like startled buttons and arranged themselves along the road, where townspeople could pick up whichever one suited the day.

Mara chose youthful, though it had no number. Pell chose jovial, which he claimed was easier to carry.

Then the orchard produced its first ordinary peach. They divided it with the engraver’s smallest blade. Inside was a wooden ladder descending through the stone.

Without deciding where it led, they climbed down.
