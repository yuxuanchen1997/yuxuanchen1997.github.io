title: "The Weather in the Barrel"
date: 2026-08-26 00:00:00

Every morning, Lio rolled the blue barrel to the edge of the village and opened its lid. Mist climbed out, unfolded its pale sleeves, and wandered among the houses. It softened the chimneys, misplaced the road, and left beads of water on every doorknob. By breakfast, the village had forgotten its own shape.

The barrel had belonged to Lio’s aunt, who said weather should never be allowed to choose its own furniture. Lio did not understand, but obeyed her penciled instructions: release the mist at seven, stir twice with a wooden spoon, and never let a kitten look inside.

For years, there had been no kitten. Then one appeared beneath the bakery cart, white as spilled flour, with one orange ear. It followed Lio everywhere, batting at loose shoelaces and sleeping in the hollow of his hat. He named it Pepper, though it smelled faintly of rain.

One morning, while Lio stirred the barrel, Pepper sprang onto the rim. The mist rose to meet it. Before Lio could blink, the kitten slipped inside, and the barrel gave a satisfied hiccup.

Lio plunged both arms into the cold whiteness. His fingers found rooftops, wet laundry, a flock of tiny bells, but no fur. Then Pepper meowed from every street at once.

The mist thickened. It filled cups, cupboards, and pockets. Villagers opened their doors and stepped into rooms that belonged to their neighbors. The baker found a bridge in his oven. The schoolteacher wrote her name on a window and watched it swim away.

Lio tipped the barrel onto its side. Out poured the hidden landscape of the mist: silver grass, upside-down fences, and hundreds of transparent kittens chasing one solid orange ear. Pepper bounded after it, seized the ear, and became whole again.

At once, the village returned to its proper arrangement—except for the bridge, which remained warm and smelled of bread.

Lio sealed the barrel. Pepper sat on the lid, purring. From then on, the mist arrived by itself, but only in small, polite patches shaped like paws. And whenever anyone crossed the bakery bridge, the road ahead briefly forgot where it was going.
