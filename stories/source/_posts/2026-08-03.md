title: "The River in the Cupboard"
date: 2026-08-03 00:00:00

Every Tuesday, Mara opened the kitchen cupboard and found a river running between the flour and the blue plates. It was narrow enough to hide behind a teacup, yet deep enough to carry a green canoe whose paddles moved without hands.

One morning, a moose stood in the canoe. He wore an apron printed with pears and balanced a glass jar between his antlers. Inside the jar, a hamster ran on a wheel, turning the river’s current.

“Your house is drifting,” said the moose.

Mara looked through the window. The neighboring roofs were sliding past like loaves on a bakery shelf. Her own house had uprooted its foundations and was floating toward the municipal swimming pool, where the mayor kept all unfinished sentences.

“Can it be stopped?” she asked.

The hamster slowed. Immediately, the spoons leaned east and the wallpaper began remembering a different room.

“Only redirected,” said the moose. He invited Mara into the canoe. She stepped over the sugar bowl and sat behind him, holding the jar steady while he paddled through the cupboard’s dark interior.

They passed islands of cinnamon, a waterfall folded inside a napkin, and a school of buttons swimming backward. Whenever the canoe approached a fork in the river, the hamster chose a direction by sneezing. Left smelled of wet newspapers; right tasted faintly of violin varnish.

At last they reached a brass tap suspended in empty air. Beneath it floated Mara’s house, small as a lunchbox. The moose instructed her to turn the tap exactly halfway.

Mara did. The river poured upward.

The canoe rose through shelves, ceilings, and a field of warm yellow umbrellas. Below them, the town rearranged itself into a question no one could pronounce. The hamster stopped running and listened.

From somewhere inside the jar came the sound of a door opening.

Mara understood. She lifted the hamster out and placed him in the canoe. Freed from the wheel, he took one paddle; the moose took the other. Together they rowed the house back toward Tuesday.

By breakfast, the cupboard was dry. Only a damp green canoe remained beside the flour, and in it, three pear seeds arranged like a map.
