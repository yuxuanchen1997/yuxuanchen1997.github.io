At seven each morning, the streets of Bellweather changed places. The bakery woke beside the aquarium; the school unfolded from beneath the tram depot; bridges exchanged rivers with the solemn efficiency of clerks. Residents survived by carrying a Guide Biscuit, issued yearly by the Bureau of Useful Things.

The biscuit was pale, square, and stamped with a tiny compass. If you whispered your destination before biting it, the crumbs fell toward the proper street. A second bite corrected mistakes. A third was discouraged. Mara, who repaired umbrellas for weather that had not happened yet, found the system useful, though the biscuits tasted of chalk and distant applause.

One Tuesday, she whispered, “My shop,” and bit. The crumbs climbed her sleeve instead of falling. They crossed her shoulder, entered her left ear, and arranged themselves behind her eyes. Suddenly she could see a narrow yellow path running through walls, kettles, and people’s unfinished sentences.

It did not lead to her shop.

Mara followed. The path passed through a laundromat where socks were learning to pair themselves, then entered the Bureau. No guard stopped her; each was busy consulting a biscuit that pointed firmly at another guard. In the basement she found a warm machine kneading dough with the city map stamped into it. Streets vanished beneath its iron palms and emerged elsewhere. Beside the machine sat an elderly guide in a green uniform, eating blank biscuits.

“You’re late,” he said.

“For what?”

“To be useful.”

He explained that the city did not rearrange naturally. The machine moved it according to the citizens’ crumbs, and the crumbs obeyed destinations supplied by the machine. It was a perfect circle, he said, except circles eventually grew hungry.

Mara looked at the yellow path. It ended at a red lever labeled IMPROVEMENT. The guide offered her the remaining half of her biscuit. “Pull first,” he advised. “Then eat.”

Instead, Mara fed the biscuit to the machine. It coughed, shuddered, and produced a door.

Beyond it stood her umbrella shop, exactly where she had left it, yet filled with rain rising from the floor in silver threads. Customers floated near the ceiling, calmly opening umbrellas beneath themselves.

Mara stepped through. Behind her, the guide asked whether this was useful.

She opened an umbrella. The upward rain carried her toward the customers, while the new door slowly chewed its own hinges.
