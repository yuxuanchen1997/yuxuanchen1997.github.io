title: "The Lettuce Ferryman"

Every Tuesday, the river climbed out of its bed and queued at the market. It bought one head of lettuce from Nara, paying with smooth blue buttons that were always warm. By noon, the water returned between its banks, leaving a damp path through the stalls. No one followed it except Orin, the town’s smallest cartographer, who suspected the river was drawing a map of somewhere that did not yet exist.

At the final bend, Orin found a turtle wearing the lettuce as a ruffled green hat. The turtle stood ankle-deep in the current, though turtles have no ankles anyone can point to. “I am carrying summer upstream,” it said. Beneath the leaves, a silver minnow flickered like a loose thought. “And I am carrying the turtle,” said the minnow, although it was scarcely longer than Orin’s thumb.

They asked Orin to guide them to the source. His maps showed a spring in the hills, but when unfolded, each sheet became a white door. They chose the third door and entered a kitchen where rain simmered in copper pans. Chairs grazed quietly on the wallpaper. At the sink, an old woman was washing names from stones and setting the clean stones aside.

“The source is thirsty,” she told them. “It has forgotten what water tastes like.” The minnow leaped into a teacup. The turtle removed its lettuce hat and peeled away leaf after leaf, laying them across the table. Each leaf became a shallow pond. The minnow swam through them in a widening spiral, stitching pond to pond with a thread of shining water. The turtle pressed its shell against the last leaf, and all the town’s Tuesday buttons began ringing underground.

By evening, the kitchen had dissolved into a green valley. A new river unrolled from the turtle’s feet, narrow as ribbon and quick with minnows. It carried the chairs, the nameless stones, and Orin’s blank maps toward the town. Orin drew no border around it. Instead, he placed one warm blue button at the map’s edge, where it sprouted into a small lettuce, crisp and whispering, while the turtle listened for summer inside its folded heart.
