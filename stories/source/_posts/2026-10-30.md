title: "The Tide’s Bakery"

Every morning, the seaside moved three steps inland, carrying its blue chairs, salt windows, and patient gulls. By Thursday it had reached Mara’s garden, where a yellow gazebo stood among tomato vines. The water stopped at its lowest stair as if waiting to be invited up.

Mara baked one muffin each day. She did not know why; the recipe had appeared in her flour jar, written on a ribbon of kelp. It called for ordinary things—oats, honey, a pinch of salt—and one sound gathered before breakfast. On Friday she folded in the click of her kettle. The muffin rose with a tiny brass handle on top.

She carried it to the gazebo. At once, the seaside climbed the stairs without wetting them. Waves settled around the railings like folded tablecloths, and small boats nosed between the chairs. The muffin began to ring. Each chime made a doorway appear in the air: first a green door opening onto a corridor of sand; then a glass door behind which fish queued politely; then a red door no larger than a postage stamp.

From that smallest door came Mara’s missing Tuesdays.

They fluttered out as paper birds, seven or eight of them, all smelling of warm dust and apricots. Mara remembered then that for years her weeks had skipped from Monday to Wednesday. She had blamed the calendar. The Tuesdays perched on the gazebo roof and demanded crumbs in voices like pencils.

Mara broke the muffin. Steam poured out and became a narrow seaside road, complete with bicycles, umbrellas, and a vendor selling jars of distance. The paper birds ate the crumbs. With every bite, one Tuesday unfolded across the garden: laundry lines filled with bright socks, neighbors began half-finished conversations, and tomatoes turned slowly in the sun to show their forgotten sides.

By afternoon, the sea retreated, embarrassed by abundance. It carried away the boats and glass door, but left its blue chairs along the fence. The gazebo kept the green door. Mara kept the red one in her pocket.

After that, she baked muffins only on Tuesdays. Sometimes she added the sound of rain entering an empty cup; sometimes the hush of a map being refolded. The seaside continued its careful wandering, but whenever it reached her garden, it paused at the gazebo and waited for a plate.
