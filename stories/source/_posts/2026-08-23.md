Every morning, the village changed color according to what Mara dreamed. On the morning of the teal rain, the roofs softened into waves, the road tasted faintly of salt, and every turnip in the market began humming through its roots.

Mara woke with moss beneath her tongue. She spat it into a cup, where it unfolded into a tiny green staircase. At the top stood a door no larger than a thumbnail. Someone knocked from the other side.

By noon, the humming turnips had rolled from their baskets and gathered outside her house. They formed a pale, patient crowd, each wearing a collar of soil. The mayor arrived carrying an umbrella, though the rain had stopped and now hung motionless in the air as teal beads.

“They say you have stolen their garden,” he told Mara.

Mara opened the little door. Beyond it stretched an orchard under a teal sky. Instead of fruit, enormous turnips dangled from silver branches, slowly turning as though listening for distant music. Moss covered the ground in thick cushions, and whenever the wind crossed it, footprints appeared—small, bare, and arriving rather than departing.

The market turnips hummed louder. Mara understood then that they were not demanding the orchard back. They wanted to enter it.

She placed the cup on the doorstep. One by one, the turnips climbed the moss staircase, squeezing through the door with soft pops. Inside, they rose like balloons and fastened themselves to empty branches. With each arrival, a forgotten object fell from the orchard: a blue button, a wooden spoon, a bell without a clapper, Mara’s first lost tooth. The villagers collected these things and recognized them, although none could remember losing them.

At sunset, only one turnip remained. It was small and crooked, with a teal thread tied around its middle. Mara lifted it, and the turnip whispered in her own voice, “Gardens are where misplaced things learn to grow.”

She planted it beside her house. Overnight, moss spread across the village, gentle as a second sleep. By morning, teal doors had appeared in every wall, and behind each one something patiently hummed, waiting to be remembered.
