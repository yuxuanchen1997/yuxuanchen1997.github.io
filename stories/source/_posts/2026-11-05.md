---
title: "The Puddle Market"
date: 2026-11-05 00:00:00
lang: en

On the first morning of the town’s newest season, Mara found a puddle waiting inside her empty cart. It was perfectly round, though the cart’s floor was made of crooked planks, and it reflected a street no one recognized: yellow balconies, blue chimneys, and laundry fluttering upward into the ground. When Mara pushed the cart, the reflected street rolled with her, keeping pace beneath the water.

At the market, customers ignored her apples and leaned over the puddle. Each person saw something new. The baker saw a loaf with windows; a child saw Tuesday folded into a paper boat; the mayor saw his office furnished with beehives. They offered coins, buttons, and promises for what they saw, but Mara could not lift anything from the water. Her hands returned carrying only a cool brightness.

By noon the puddle began to whisper directions. Left at the fountain shaped like a question. Right where the cobbler sold shoes to tables. Straight until the houses forget their numbers. Mara obeyed. The cart squeaked through lanes that grew narrower and newer, their bricks still soft as bread. Behind her, townspeople followed, hoping the puddle would invent better lives for them.

At last the cart stopped before a blank wall. In the puddle, however, a green door stood open. The whisper said, “Deliver the cart.”

Mara tipped the handles. Instead of spilling, the puddle rose in one shining sheet and leaned against the wall. The reflected door became deep enough to enter. One by one the customers climbed through, carrying nothing except the new expressions on their faces. The baker looked surprised; the child looked older; the mayor buzzed faintly. No one came back.

When the last coat vanished beyond the green frame, the doorway folded itself into the cart. The puddle was gone. In its place lay a small dry street map, showing every familiar road except the one Mara stood on. She turned the cart around, but the wheels had become compass needles, each pointing toward a different beginning.

Mara laughed, because the town behind her had already started repainting itself. Then she chose the wheel that trembled most and pushed.
