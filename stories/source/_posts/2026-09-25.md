At dawn, Mara found the tractor idling inside the linen cupboard. Its red hood pressed between shelves of folded sheets, and its exhaust smelled of starch and pears. A note hung from the steering wheel: THE FIELD HAS WRINKLED. PLEASE IRON BEFORE NOON. Beyond the kitchen window, the wheat lay in immense striped pleats, gold alternating with green, as if the earth had dressed hurriedly for an appointment.

Mara climbed aboard. The tractor reversed politely through the pantry, scattering buttons instead of crumbs, then rolled outside. Each furrow it crossed rose behind it as a ribbon of pale linen. Larks landed on the cloth and became embroidered commas. Mara tried steering toward the hill, but the striped field kept rearranging itself beneath the tires, putting the barn on the left, then underneath, then briefly inside her coat pocket.

At the field’s center stood a scarecrow in a striped suit, holding an iron the size of a door. “You’re late,” it said, though every clock on the farm showed a different vegetable. “The wrinkles have learned to migrate. If they reach the road, travelers will arrive before they leave.”

Mara asked what could stop them. The scarecrow pointed to the tractor’s plow. During their journey, it had become a silver needle. Together they threaded the linen furrows through its eye. Mara drove in widening circles while the scarecrow pressed the iron down. Steam filled the air with rooms that had never been built: a blue nursery, a corridor full of spoons, a library where every book contained the same small door.

Whenever the tractor crossed a golden stripe, the field hummed. Whenever it crossed a green one, the linen tightened. Mara continued until the land lay smooth and flat, with one stubborn crease remaining along the horizon. “Leave it,” said the scarecrow. “A perfectly ironed world has nowhere to hide its mistakes.”

By noon, the tractor had shrunk to the size of a thimble. Mara placed it in her pocket beside the barn and walked home across the quiet stripes. In the cupboard, every sheet bore a map of the farm, except one. It showed a road unwinding from her front door into a country shaped like a wrinkle. At its border, a tiny red tractor waited with its engine running.
