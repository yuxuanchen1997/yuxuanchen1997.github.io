---
title: "The Thirst Field"
date: 2026-11-11 00:00:00
lang: en

---

At sunrise, Mara found a blue jug standing in the east field, though the earth had been plowed flat the night before. It was taller than her knee and warm. When she pulled its cork, she heard a tractor idling inside, no louder than a trapped bee. Her tractor, parked by the barn, answered with a cough.

The village suffered a peculiar drought: water still ran, but refused to make anything wet. Rain rolled from roofs in silver beads and bounced back into the clouds. Mara drank three glasses each morning and remained thirsty. She carried the jug to the barn, where forty saved sheets of cardboard were stamped THIS SIDE UP in several languages.

Following no instruction she could explain, Mara built a cardboard road across the field. She laid each sheet edge to edge, weighting corners with potatoes. The tractor climbed onto it willingly. As she drove, the road folded itself behind the rear wheels and unfolded ahead, a brown ribbon traveling without advancing. The jug rode beside her. With every circuit, the engine inside it grew louder, while the tractor became quieter, until their sounds exchanged places.

At noon, the cardboard road turned upward. Mara did not brake. Tractor and jug ascended at a mild angle through the bright air, passing laundry lines, chimney pots, and a flock of spoons migrating north. Below, villagers pointed with empty cups. Mara tipped the jug. Instead of water, a small red tractor poured out, then hundreds more, tumbling gently downward on parachutes made from grocery receipts.

Where each landed, its wheels pressed a dark furrow into the dust. From those furrows rose clear walls of water, upright as glass fences. Children opened gates in them and led streams home by the hand. Mara's tractor, now silent, continued along the cardboard sky until the last sheet softened. She stepped onto the blue jug, which floated. The tractor folded itself into a square, stamped THIS SIDE UP, and slipped into her pocket.

By evening, wetness had returned to the village. Mara came down in the east field, carrying the jug. It was empty except for the smell of diesel and rain. When she set it down, the soil took a long drink. Beneath her boots, a tiny horn sounded twice, asking for the road.
