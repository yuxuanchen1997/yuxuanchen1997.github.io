---
title: "The Fence That Learned Surprise"
date: 2026-08-19 00:00:00
lang: en

---

Every morning, Mara painted the fence blue, and every afternoon the fence forgot.

It stood across the salt meadow in a patient zigzag, dividing nothing from nothing. By noon its boards had returned to the pale color of bread dough. Mara would press one ear against them and hear small domestic sounds: spoons entering drawers, kettles reconsidering water, shoes whispering about roads.

On the seventh day, an ibis landed on the highest post. It was taller than any ibis had a right to be, with red legs jointed like folding rulers. From beneath one wing it drew a silver key and tapped the fence three times.

A door appeared.

“Surprising,” Mara said, because the word escaped before she could choose a better one.

The ibis tilted its long beak. “Surprise is not a feeling,” it replied. “It is a place people reach too quickly.”

Then it unlocked the door.

Beyond the fence lay Mara’s kitchen, though the meadow remained visible through its windows. A loaf of bread sat on the table, breathing gently. Six teacups circled it in solemn little steps. On the wall hung a portrait of the fence, painted blue. In the portrait, Mara stood on the opposite side, holding a brush made from ibis feathers.

She entered. At once, the real fence shuddered across the meadow. Each board turned a different color—mustard, violet, peach, the green of a question not yet asked. The ibis followed and closed the door behind them.

“Now,” it said, “you must decide which side is outside.”

Mara looked through the kitchen window. In the meadow, another Mara was painting the fence blue. Her strokes were careful and familiar. When she finished, she placed the brush on the grass and listened to the boards.

From inside the wall came the clink of Mara’s six cups.

The second Mara smiled, as if she had heard good news.

This was surprising, but Mara kept the word behind her teeth. She opened the breathing loaf. Inside was a warm, narrow path leading between fields of blue grass.

The ibis stepped onto it first.

Mara followed, carrying one teacup. Behind her, the fence remembered every color at once.
