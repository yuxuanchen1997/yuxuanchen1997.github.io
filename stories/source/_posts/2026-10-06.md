title: "What Is Trying to Open"

Behind Mira’s apartment building stood a shed no tenant remembered building. It was thin as a book seen edge-on, painted the yellow of old receipts. Each morning, its door opened one finger’s width and released a smell of pencil shavings. By noon it closed again, though there was never any wind in the courtyard.

One morning, Mira carried down the screwdriver she used to tighten the loose handles of her life: the kitchen drawer, the radio dial, the small brass nameplate on her door. The shed admitted her sideways. Inside, shelves rose like lines in a ruled notebook, holding jars labeled TUESDAY, DISTANCE, and THE PART OF A SONG YOU HUM INCORRECTLY.

At the back, a silver screw protruded from the wall. It was so thin that she mistook it for a crack in the air. When she set the screwdriver against it, the tool turned by itself. With every rotation, the courtyard outside narrowed. Laundry lines became pencil strokes; windows flattened into blue stamps; voices slipped beneath doors like envelopes.

Mira tried to reverse the screwdriver, but its handle had grown soft and warm, like bread. The labeled jars began whispering their contents. Tuesday complained of being overused. Distance asked to be measured in spoons. The unfinished song poured from its jar as a ribbon of green sound and wound around Mira’s wrist. It tugged her toward a narrow cupboard she had not noticed.

Within the cupboard stood a second shed, smaller and thinner, with a door exactly her height. Through it she saw her apartment, but every object had become useful in a different way. Chairs caught rain. Plates told time. Her kettle patiently ironed a cloud. On the table lay another screwdriver, and beside it a note in her own handwriting: DO NOT TIGHTEN WHAT IS TRYING TO OPEN.

Mira released the handle. The silver screw sprang free and scuttled into a mouse hole. At once the courtyard regained its width, yet the shed remained thin. She stepped outside carrying nothing but the green ribbon, which had gone silent. Upstairs, every loose handle in her apartment was turning slowly, not falling off, but making room.
