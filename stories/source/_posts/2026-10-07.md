---
title: "Weather Behind the Curtain"
date: 2026-10-07 00:00:00

Every morning, Mara opened the blue curtain at the back of her bakery and checked the weather hanging behind it. The curtain did not cover a window. It covered a shallow cupboard where ordinary flour should have been. Yet since the Tuesday when the clocks began smelling of oranges, a small storm had lived there, revolving over a sandy plain no wider than a baking tray.

She fed it crumbs. Thunder softened the dough, and lightning gave the loaves delicate blue veins. Customers said Mara’s bread tasted of journeys they had postponed. After eating it, they discovered sand in their shoes and tiny striped umbrellas opening inside their coat pockets. Mara swept the sand into jars, labeled it THURSDAY, and stacked it beside the cinnamon.

One afternoon, the storm refused its crumbs. It pushed a hot wind through the curtain, scattering receipts like pale birds. The sandy plain had risen into dunes; among them stood a red door Mara had never seen. Someone on the other side knocked three times, patiently, as if waiting for bread to finish baking.

Mara tied the curtain around her waist and stepped into the cupboard. At once the bakery became far away, a warm square floating above the dunes. The storm circled her ankles, sulking. She crossed to the red door and opened it. Behind it was her bakery again, but abandoned to an afternoon that had not yet happened. On the counter lay a single loaf shaped like her own hand.

When she lifted it, the loaf whispered, “Close early.” Mara disliked advice from food, but the storm had begun folding the horizon into sharp pleats. She hurried back. The red door followed, walking on its hinges. Dunes poured after it, filling the cupboard, then the bakery, then the street, until every doorstep wore a sandy tongue.

Mara pulled the blue curtain across the front window. The instant its hem touched the floor, the storm went silent. Sand rose from the street and streamed beneath the cloth, grain by grain. The red door bowed and slipped after it. By sunset, the town was clean.

Only Mara’s bakery remained closed. Behind its curtain, people heard cups clinking, rain applauding, and a patient knock from somewhere that might have been tomorrow.
