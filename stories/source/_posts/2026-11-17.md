At nine each morning, Mara carried a blue canvas bag into the municipal office, although nobody had hired her and the building had no door. She entered by unfolding a crease in the brickwork. Inside, clerks sat at desks made of warm bread, stamping yesterday’s weather onto sheets of glass. Mara’s bag contained a single narcissus, roots wrapped in a legal brief whose pages continued deeper than the bag itself.

The brief concerned a dispute between the town and its river. The town claimed the river had abandoned its proper channel; the river replied, in blue handwriting, that the town had wandered away during sleep. Mara had been appointed to carry the evidence to the courthouse before noon. Yet whenever she tried to read the final paragraph, the narcissus lifted its yellow face and breathed a small wind across the words, rearranging guilt into gilt, boundary into bakery, law into lawn.

She crossed the square, where citizens were practicing their reflections in empty shop windows. From the bag came the sound of shallow water and a distant judge clearing her throat. Mara looked inside. Beneath the flower lay a staircase descending through paragraphs. On its lowest visible step, the river waited in a gray coat, holding the town folded under one arm like a map.

“Which of you is telling the truth?” Mara asked.

The river removed its hat. Rain rose from it toward the ceiling of the bag. “Truth is the place that remains when both parties stop pointing.”

The narcissus nodded so vigorously that its petals became small yellow boats. They sailed down the staircase, each carrying one sentence from the brief. As the sentences touched water, commas opened like gates, and the river climbed out through the punctuation.

By noon the square was flowing gently. The courthouse loosened its steps and drifted beside a fruit stall. Clerks launched glass pages from the windows. Mara set the bag on the current and watched it travel toward whichever shore was listening. Only the narcissus remained in her hand, its roots clean of argument. When she planted it in a crack in the square, every compass in town turned toward the flower, and for one brief afternoon, nobody mistook direction for justice.
