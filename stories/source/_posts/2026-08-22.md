title: "The Measure of Rain"
date: 2026-08-22 00:00:00

Every morning, Ivo crossed the courtyard with a brass ruler tucked beneath his arm. The courtyard belonged to no building. Four yellow walls surrounded it, each painted with a door that opened only when nobody watched. At its center stood a dry fountain where seven stone fish waited for water.

Ivo’s work was to measure the rain before it fell.

He placed the ruler upright in the fountain and read the tiny numbers engraved along its edge. Some days they predicted three centimeters; some days, a staircase; once, a flock of blue spoons. Whatever the ruler announced arrived by noon. Ivo recorded each result in a ledger and locked the ledger inside a small green box.

The lock had begun to complain.

“You keep everything that happens,” it said, “but nothing that almost happens.”

Ivo considered this unfair. Almost-rain could not be measured. Almost-doors could not be entered. Still, the next morning, the ruler showed no numbers at all. Along its edge ran a single narrow keyhole.

He carried it to the green box. The lock clicked its tongue. “At last.”

Ivo inserted the ruler. It turned softly, bending like a ribbon without breaking. The box opened, but the ledger was gone. Inside lay the courtyard, folded to the size of a handkerchief. Its walls breathed in slow pleats. The fountain trembled with a silver drop that refused to fall.

Ivo reached in and unfolded the courtyard across the courtyard.

For a moment there were two of everything: two Ivors, two fountains, fourteen fish, eight painted doors. Then the duplicate doors swung open, though both Ivors were watching. Behind each stood an event that had nearly occurred: rain rising from puddles into clouds, spoons returning to drawers, strangers remembering one another before meeting.

The two Ivors placed the ruler between them. It measured neither height nor distance now, but choice. One Ivo stepped through a door. The other remained beside the fountain.

At noon, the silver drop finally fell upward. The lock laughed inside its empty box, and every painted door became a window.

Through one of them, Ivo could see himself walking away, growing smaller and more exact, until he was only a mark on the ruler.
