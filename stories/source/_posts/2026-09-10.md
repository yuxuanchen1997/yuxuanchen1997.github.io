At noon, the gale arrived wearing a waiter’s white jacket. It pushed open every door in Bellweather Café and set the teaspoons ringing against their saucers.

“Your order,” it said to Mara, although she had ordered nothing.

From its sleeve, the gale produced a folded napkin. A map was drawn on it in mustard: three streets, a blue staircase, and a small green X that moved whenever Mara blinked. Beside the sugar bowl, a caterpillar lifted its head and studied the map with professional concern.

“That is my house,” the caterpillar said. “Or it will be, if we reach it before the cloth is washed.”

The gale seized the napkin and rushed outside. Mara followed, carrying the caterpillar in a teaspoon. They crossed streets that pleated themselves like fans. The painted staircase rose from a puddle, each step soft and blue as cheese. At the top stood a field of tables, all set for guests who had not yet decided what shape to take.

The green X crawled toward the napkin’s corner. The caterpillar trembled. “Faster. The laundry is beginning.”

Rain fell upward. Soap bubbles rolled across the sky. The gale, suddenly ashamed of its strength, tried to shelter the napkin beneath its jacket, but its buttons were tiny revolving doors, and the bubbles slipped through.

Mara placed the napkin on the largest table and pinned its corners with cups. The caterpillar climbed onto the green X. At once, the mustard streets thickened into hedges, the blue staircase hardened, and a little house unfolded from the cloth, complete with windows, chimney, and a bell made from a thimble.

“You may visit,” said the caterpillar, entering through a buttonhole, “but only while you are uncertain.”

The gale bowed. Mara blinked, and the field became the café again. On her table lay a clean white napkin. Under it, something small was chewing a doorway.

Outside, the wind waited politely, holding a tray.
