Every morning, the town woke misty, though no river crossed it and no rain had fallen for years. The mist came from the kitchen taps. It curled from kettles, filled cupboards, and left small fingerprints on the bread. By breakfast, everyone had misplaced something: a spoon, a name, the reason for wearing shoes.

Only Mara the plumber understood that the pipes were dreaming. Pressing her ear to the walls, she heard copper forests trembling beneath the streets. Faucets whispered of roots; drains complained of the sky. One Tuesday, the mayor’s bathtub began producing acorns, each engraved with tomorrow’s date.

Mara opened the floor with her silver wrench. A chipmunk climbed out, wearing a blue thread around its waist like a workman’s belt. It bowed, then unrolled a map drawn on onion skin. The map showed the town upside down, with every house hanging from a vast underground branch.

“You’ve tightened the weather too far,” said the chipmunk. Its voice sounded like pebbles poured into a cup. “The mist has nowhere to go.”

Together they descended through the pipe. Mara carried her wrench; the chipmunk carried three acorns and a thimble of salt. Below the town, plumbing spread in shining loops around an enormous sleeping tree. Its leaves were porcelain basins, its fruit brass valves. Mist pulsed inside its trunk, thick with forgotten errands and unfinished sentences.

At the lowest root, they found a red wheel rusted shut. Mara pulled until her hands rang like bells. The chipmunk cracked an acorn against the wheel, and tomorrow spilled out: warm light, bicycle music, the smell of green paint. The rust softened. The wheel turned.

Above them, every tap sneezed once. Mist rushed backward through the pipes and gathered beneath the earth, becoming a clear, quiet lake among the roots. By noon, the town remembered its missing things.

Mara returned to her kitchen. On the table lay her silver wrench and the onion-skin map. The chipmunk was gone. Yet when she turned on the tap, one blue thread flowed into her palm, dry as a road, pointing somewhere below.
