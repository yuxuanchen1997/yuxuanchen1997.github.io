title: "The Forest Takes Attendance"

On the first warm Tuesday of every month, the woodland rearranged its paths into the shape of a question mark. Mara, who kept records for the village council, entered carrying a clipboard and a silver spoon. The clipboard held a list of things the woodland had borrowed: three fence shadows, the smell of fresh bread, a child's talent for whistling, and all the blue from Mr. Vale's front door.

At a clearing shaped like an ear, Mara struck the clipboard with the spoon. The sound was small but official. Trees leaned closer; mushrooms raised their pale caps as if removing hats.

“Item one,” Mara announced. “Fence shadows.”

A badger rolled out a bundle of striped darkness and placed it at her feet. She checked a box.

“Item two: bread.”

The leaves exhaled together. The clearing filled with the warm scent of a bakery that had never owned an oven.

But when she asked for the whistle, the woodland became busy pretending to be ordinary. Beetles inspected bark. A stream revised its reflection. Somewhere, a branch practiced being still.

Mara tapped the spoon against her teeth. Each note opened a tiny drawer in the air. From one drawer flew a yellow tune, twisting like ribbon above the ferns. It refused the clipboard, circling Mara's head until she laughed. The laugh startled the list: its ink loosened and crawled into a new sentence.

RETURN THE THING YOU BORROWED, it read.

Mara searched her pockets. She found two buttons, a bus ticket to a town with no streets, and an acorn polished smooth by worry. None belonged to the woodland. She looked at the spoon. It had come from her office drawer, though she could not remember putting it there.

The spoon trembled in her hand and bent toward the soil like a compass. Beneath a lace of roots lay a narrow table set for one. On it waited a bowl filled with green silence. The spoon leapt down and began to eat.

With every mouthful, a path vanished from the question mark until only a straight line remained, pointing home. The woodland returned the blue last: it poured from a knothole and followed Mara like a patient ribbon.

At the village edge, she found her clipboard blank except for one checked box labeled MARA. She carried it home without deciding whether she had been returned or borrowed.
