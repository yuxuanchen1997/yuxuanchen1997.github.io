title: "The Briar Flight"
date: 2026-11-22 00:00:00

On the first warm Tuesday of winter, an airplane landed in Mara’s vegetable garden. It was no larger than a wheelbarrow, though its four windows showed rows of passengers settling into blue seats. The propeller turned with the patient rhythm of a spoon stirring soup. Around the landing gear, the briar hedge unfastened its thorns and climbed aboard.

Mara approached wearing the woolen coat her aunt had knitted from the fleece of imaginary sheep. Whenever she buttoned it, distant bells answered from places that had no towers. The pilot leaned from the cockpit and asked whether she had anything to declare. Mara searched her pockets. She found a warm pebble, three teaspoons of rain, and a letter addressed to Tomorrow in her own handwriting.

“The briar has reserved every seat,” said the pilot. Through the windows, green canes curled around the armrests, flowering into small white tickets. “But it refuses to say where it is going.”

Mara understood. For years the hedge had circled the garden, watching cabbages become cabbages and paths return faithfully to the gate. It had grown tired of being a border. She removed her woolen coat and spread it across the garden like a runway. Its sleeves lengthened over the hills; its red stitching became lights. The airplane shivered with delight.

The briar drew Mara inside by one gentle thorn. There was no room, yet the cabin expanded whenever she breathed. The pilot offered her a compass whose needle pointed toward whatever had most recently been forgotten. It swung past north and settled on the unopened letter.

They rose. Below them, Mara’s coat remained stretched across the fields, keeping the earth warm. The airplane flew through a cloud shaped like an empty cupboard, then over a river carrying thousands of upside-down doors. Each time the propeller turned, the briar blossomed in a different season.

Mara opened the letter. It contained only a garden key and one sentence: Please leave the gate where the sky can find it.

So she dropped the key. Far below, it became a bright green gate standing alone in a wheat field. The sky descended carefully, opened it, and entered. After that, there was enough blue inside the airplane for everyone to have a window.
