Every morning, the town changed texture. On Monday, the streets felt like warm bread; on Tuesday, every wall bristled with green feathers. Citizens carried sample cards in their pockets and rubbed them against doorways before entering. Only Mara’s kitchen remained stubbornly smooth, except for the peeler in the blue drawer.

The peeler was lively in a way no utensil should be. It clicked its metal tongue at sunrise, shuffled among the spoons, and sprang into Mara’s hand whenever the town bell rang. Mara had inherited it with one instruction scratched into its handle: PEEL ONLY WHAT ASKS.

For years, nothing asked. Then the town awoke with the texture of an orange. Houses dimpled; chimneys smelled bright and bitter. The courthouse had grown a tiny green stem. In the square, people pressed their ears to the pavement and heard juice moving under the cobbles.

“Peel us,” whispered the town.

The peeler leaped from Mara’s drawer and dragged her outside. Its blade flashed eagerly toward the nearest wall, but Mara held it back. “Why?” she asked.

“We are tired of being one thing all the way through.”

So Mara began at the bakery. The peeler skimmed beneath the rough plaster, releasing a ribbon of rind as long as a parade. Underneath was not brick but a lively interior landscape: red ladders climbing sideways, ponds folded like handkerchiefs, and small winds asleep in jars. The baker stepped through his wall and returned carrying a blue gust that smelled of cinnamon.

House by house, Mara peeled the town. Each surface opened onto something impossible yet familiar. The school contained an orchard of unfinished questions. The fountain concealed a room where rain rehearsed its falling. Beneath Mara’s own smooth kitchen walls, she found countless hands kneading light into loaves.

By evening, only the courthouse remained. It trembled and begged, but the peeler had become dull from wonder. Mara pressed its handle to the building. “You must ask it yourself.”

The courthouse bent its stem. The peeler gave one exhausted click and carved a narrow window. Through it, everyone saw the next morning waiting—soft, lively, and covered in scales. No one slept. They spent the night sewing gentler pockets for their sample cards.
