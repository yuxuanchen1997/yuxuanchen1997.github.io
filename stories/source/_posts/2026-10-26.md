---
title: "The Avenue That Turned Aside"
date: 2026-10-26 00:00:00
lang: en

---

At seven each morning, Bellweather Avenue unfastened itself from the city and wandered east. The houses remained behind, surprised but polite, while the pavement slid beneath sleeping pigeons and carried shops, lampposts, and one blue mailbox through fields of mustard flowers. By noon, the avenue usually returned with dust on its curbs and nothing to explain.

Mara lived above the bakery and measured these excursions with a kitchen saucer. She placed it on the windowsill before sunrise. When the avenue moved, the saucer trembled and filled with whatever weather lay ahead: rainwater, warm sand, once a small cloud folded like a handkerchief. Mara kept notes, though the saucer never repeated itself.

One Tuesday it filled with green feathers. Beyond the last apartment block, a white fence appeared across the road. It stretched from one horizon to the other, without gate or corner. Bellweather Avenue stopped so abruptly that every doorbell rang.

The fence spoke through its pickets. “No street may pass unless it leaves its destination behind.”

The avenue shuddered. Its destination was the city itself—the bakery, the bus stop, the children chalking arithmetic on the curb. Without that destination, it would become only a strip of asphalt wandering among possibilities. Yet it could not reverse. The fence had already grown behind it, enclosing the avenue in a narrow country where all clocks showed seven.

Mara carried the saucer downstairs. The green feathers had arranged themselves into a tiny map. At its center was a blank circle. She set the saucer on the road and nudged it forward. It rolled on its rim toward the fence, gathering speed, until it slipped between two pickets.

Immediately, the fence began shrinking. Its endless boards folded inward, neat as a fan, and settled inside the saucer. The blank circle on the feather-map became a gate.

Bellweather Avenue returned home that evening by a route no one remembered taking. Afterward, it stayed attached to the city. But each morning Mara found the saucer farther along the windowsill, facing east, with a short white fence growing around its rim and a faint road continuing through the glaze.
