Every morning, the aspen grove received a present. It arrived before the birds, wrapped in plain paper and tied with blue thread. No one saw who placed it beneath the whitest tree. At sunrise, the leaves applauded, and Mira, Keeper of New Things, walked out from town with her brass scissors.

The rule was simple: the present must be opened exactly when the courthouse clock struck Now. Inside would be something new—a flavor, a staircase, a minor law of balance—and Mira would carry it back for everyone. Thus the town acquired sideways rain, spoons that remembered soup, and a second Wednesday that appeared only in empty rooms.

One autumn morning, there was no parcel. The grove stood rigid, each aspen leaf holding its pale underside toward the sky. The clock struck Now, but the sound did not travel. Bakers remained bent above their dough. A dog waited forever for its lifted paw to descend. Only Mira could move, because her office required her to stand slightly ahead of the present.

She searched among the trees and found a blue thread disappearing into a split trunk. When she pulled it, the grove folded inward like an umbrella. At its center sat a small unopened box, warm as bread. The label bore Mira’s own handwriting: FOR THE FIRST PERSON WHO HAS NEVER BEEN NEW.

Mira thought of the town, where every child was introduced as a novelty and every old cup was praised for surviving its replacements. She thought of herself, appointed at birth and therefore never surprised by her own arrival. Then she cut the thread.

Inside lay a single aspen leaf, green on both sides. It lifted itself into the still air and began turning. With each turn, the town loosened: dough fell, the paw touched earth, rain remembered downward. Yet Mira felt something unfamiliar opening behind her face, a door without hinges. She returned carrying nothing visible.

From then on, the grove sent no more parcels. Instead, each morning the townspeople brought it something they believed they knew—a key, a name, a handful of flour. Beneath the aspens, each object became briefly unrecognizable. The clock continued striking Now, though sometimes, just afterward, it sounded like a question.
