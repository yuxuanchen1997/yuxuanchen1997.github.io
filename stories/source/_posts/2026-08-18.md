title: "The Stripe That Arrived"

Every Tuesday, the town received the present.

It appeared at noon in the dry fountain: a box wrapped in white paper, tied with a green ribbon, addressed simply TO WHOEVER IS MISSING. No one remembered establishing the custom, yet everyone gathered to watch the mayor lift the lid.

Inside there was always parsley.

Not ordinary parsley, although it smelled sharply of kitchens and rain. Its stems were arranged like tiny roads, and along each leaf ran a silver stripe. When the mayor touched one, the stripe slipped onto his finger and continued up his sleeve. Soon it crossed his jacket, the fountain, and the square, narrow as a shoelace and bright as a thought one almost understands.

The townspeople followed.

The stripe entered the bakery, circled a loaf, and departed through the keyhole. It climbed a school wall, underlining a child’s unfinished sentence. It crossed six gardens, where the parsley bowed as if recognizing an official procession. At last it reached the house of Mara Vale, who had not opened her door for eleven years because the rooms kept changing places.

Mara was waiting inside with an empty vase.

“You’re late,” she told the stripe.

The line rose from the floor and wrapped itself around the vase. At once, every house in town gave a small, cupboard-like sigh. Doors became windows; staircases remembered forgotten landings. In Mara’s kitchen, the ceiling lowered until she could brush it with her palm, and a blue room—missing since her childhood—unfolded behind the stove.

At its center stood a table set for one. On the plate lay a sprig of silver-striped parsley.

Mara placed it in the vase. The leaves began whispering all the names the town had misplaced: names of alleys, recipes, pets, songs, and one bridge dismantled before anyone had crossed it. People outside repeated the names, uncertain whether they were remembering or inventing.

By evening, the stripe faded from the streets. The mayor carried the empty present box back to the fountain.

The next Tuesday, nothing arrived.

Instead, every doorstep held a parcel wrapped in white paper. Each bore the same address, written in Mara’s careful hand:

TO WHOEVER IS PRESENT.
