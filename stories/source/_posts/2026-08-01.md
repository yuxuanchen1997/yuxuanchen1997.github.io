Every Tuesday, the local weather arrived in a green cart and parked beside the bakery. Mr. Vale, who had been elected to keep it orderly, unfolded the clouds, polished the wind, and measured the rain into blue glass jars. The villagers brought baskets, umbrellas, and small complaints.

One morning, the cart contained only a single herb growing from a porcelain cup. Its leaves were shaped like tiny open doors. Whenever someone touched one, a merry tune escaped—not from the plant, but from the nearest object. The lamppost sang through its bolts. A loaf of bread whistled from its crust. Three bicycles harmonized so sweetly that their riders forgot where they were going.

“This is not weather,” said Mr. Vale, consulting the regulations.

The herb leaned toward him. His clipboard began to dance.

By noon, the whole square had become dangerously cheerful. Shoes tapped without permission. Windows blinked in rhythm. Even the stern fountain tossed its water upward in the shapes of laughing commas. Mr. Vale feared the merriment would spread beyond the local boundary, where weather belonged to other officials and joy required stamped documents.

He carried the herb to the edge of town, intending to bury it beneath the sign that read YOU ARE NOW ELSEWHERE. But the road curled back like a ribbon, returning him to the square. He tried again. Each time, the town rearranged itself politely around him: the bakery exchanged places with the school, alleys tied themselves into bows, and chimneys stepped aside on brick feet.

At sunset, exhausted, Mr. Vale sat beside the green cart. The herb opened one leaf-door. Inside was a room no larger than a thimble, where a miniature Mr. Vale carefully bottled rain and frowned at music.

Mr. Vale laughed. It was a rusty, astonished sound. At once, the village fell silent.

The next Tuesday, the local weather arrived as usual. Mr. Vale unfolded a mild storm, distributed six jars of drizzle, and placed the herb on his desk. From then on, whenever the regulations grew too heavy, he stroked one leaf. Somewhere nearby, something ordinary remembered its hidden instrument.
