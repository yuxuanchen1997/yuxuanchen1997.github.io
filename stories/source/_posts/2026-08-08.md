title: "The Pale Correspondence"

Every Tuesday, a letter appeared beneath Ilya’s kitchen table. It bore no stamp, no address, only a pale thumbprint that faded whenever he tried to examine it. Inside, a single sentence was written in green ink: Please tighten the afternoon.

For three weeks Ilya ignored it. Consequently, the hours after lunch grew loose. Chairs drifted several inches from their shadows. Tea took too long to reach the bottom of a cup. At four o’clock, people in the street repeated their last gestures, waving twice, opening doors they had already entered.

On the fourth Tuesday, the letter contained a small screwdriver. Its handle was warm and translucent, with a yellow bubble suspended inside. Beneath it, the message read: Begin where the light rattles.

Ilya listened throughout the apartment. The refrigerator hummed politely; the pipes clicked in their sleep. At last he heard a faint metallic trembling behind the pale rectangle of sunlight on the pantry wall. When he touched the screwdriver to it, a screwhead surfaced in the brightness.

He turned it clockwise. Outside, a bus completed its journey without stretching like taffy between stops. A woman’s dropped orange fell directly to the pavement instead of pausing to consider the window of a bakery. Encouraged, Ilya tightened three more screws hidden in the afternoon. Each turn made the room more exact, but less familiar. The table forgot a stain shaped like an island. The kettle lost the whistle it had borrowed from a train. His own reflection began arriving in mirrors precisely on time.

Then he found the final screw in the letter itself, set neatly through the pale thumbprint. The green sentence had changed: Tighten only if you prefer an answer.

Ilya held the page against the window. Behind it, hundreds of letters moved through the sky like transparent fish, each carrying a screwdriver to some uncertain room. He understood that the looseness was not damage. It was space—small, necessary space—for objects to misremember their duties and for afternoons to become larger than clocks.

He turned the final screw counterclockwise.

At once, the letter unfolded into a door. Beyond it waited a kitchen almost like his, where Tuesday sat at the table, pale and patient, writing him another letter.
