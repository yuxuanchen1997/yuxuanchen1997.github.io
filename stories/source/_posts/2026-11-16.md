title: "Cut Along the Bark"

Every morning, the sea arrived at Mara’s kitchen wearing a blue apron. It poured itself across the tiles, filled the cups, and left a coconut on the table. The coconut was always warm. When Mara pressed her ear to its husk, she heard a dog barking from a great distance, though the sound seemed to come from inside her own cupboard.

One Tuesday, the sea brought scissors instead. They were long and silver, with handles shaped like sleeping fish. A paper label tied to them read: CUT ALONG THE BARK.

Mara’s dog, Pepper, had never barked. He communicated by sneezing small white feathers, which Mara swept into jars. But when she showed him the scissors, he opened his mouth and released a single blue ribbon of sound. It unrolled through the kitchen, slipped beneath the door, and continued down the street.

Mara followed, carrying the coconut under one arm and the scissors in her free hand. Pepper trotted ahead, barking the ribbon longer. It crossed the bakery, where loaves turned to watch, and circled the dry fountain three times before climbing an invisible stairway above the market.

At the top, the ribbon ended in a knot. Mara cut it.

The sky opened like cloth. Behind it was another kitchen, upside down and brightly lit. A woman resembling Mara stood on its ceiling, holding a silent dog and a pair of wooden scissors. Between them hung an enormous coconut, green as an unopened thought.

“Which side is real?” the other Mara asked.

Pepper sneezed. A white feather drifted upward—or downward—and pierced the coconut. Milk rained into both kitchens. Wherever it touched, ordinary things remembered their secret shapes: spoons became narrow boats; chairs unfolded into patient ladders; the sea took off its apron and curled beneath the table like a tired animal.

Mara closed the scissors. “Both sides are hungry,” she said.

Together, the two dogs began to drink. The great coconut shrank until it fit in Mara’s palm. The cut sky stitched itself shut with the blue ribbon, leaving one loose thread.

That evening, Mara tied the thread around Pepper’s collar. At last he barked—not loudly, but enough to make every jar of feathers hatch into doors. Mara opened one. Beyond it, the next morning waited barefoot, holding a coconut and asking to be let in.
