title: "The Balcony That Bloomed"
date: 2026-08-11 00:00:00

Every morning, Mara’s balcony moved one floor higher.

At first she blamed the elevator, then the numbered doors, then the building itself, which had recently begun sighing through its pipes. But on Tuesday she leaned over the railing and saw her neighbor’s red laundry fluttering three stories below, where yesterday it had been level with her hands.

Only the lilac remained unchanged. It grew from a blue pot in the corner, though Mara had never planted it. Its roots made a faint ticking sound beneath the soil. Each time the balcony rose, another cluster opened, filling the air with a fragrance like a forgotten room.

On Thursday, a kitten appeared among the blossoms. It was no larger than Mara’s glove, with gray fur and lilac-colored eyes. When she reached for it, the kitten stepped backward into the plant and vanished. A moment later, a meow drifted from somewhere above.

Mara climbed onto a chair. Beyond the highest flowers, she found a narrow staircase woven from stems. The kitten waited on the first step, washing one paw with grave concentration. Then it turned and climbed.

They passed balconies where spoons nested in flower boxes, where umbrellas grew feathers, where an old man ironed maps until the countries became smooth and nameless. No one seemed surprised to see them.

At the top stood a balcony without a building. It floated in warm blue air, carrying only a white door and a lilac bush taller than a tree. The kitten scratched the door.

Mara opened it and entered her own kitchen.

Everything was almost correct. The kettle whispered on the stove. Her cup held a small cloud instead of tea. Through the window, she saw the balcony far below, descending gently past the floors it had borrowed.

The kitten jumped onto the table and dropped a lilac blossom beside her hand. Inside its tiny throat, the petals held a staircase, a railing, and a woman climbing after a gray animal.

Mara placed the blossom in her cup. The cloud turned purple and began to rain upward.

By breakfast, the balcony had returned to its usual floor. Yet whenever the kitten purred, the blue pot ticked faster, as if counting the rooms still hidden above them.
