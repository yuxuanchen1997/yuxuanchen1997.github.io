title: "The Pouch of Morning"

In Bellweather, morning did not rise. It was delivered. Each day at six, a blue wallaby hopped down the dry riverbed with morning folded inside her pouch: a warm, wrinkled cloth smelling of toast and wet stone. At the first yucca, she stopped, pulled out one golden corner, and shook. Light spread over the town’s roofs. Kettles began to whistle. Names returned to the shop signs.

One Tuesday, the yucca closed its white blossoms like a fist. The wallaby tugged, but morning had snagged on the plant’s hooked leaves. Only a narrow stripe escaped. It illuminated one side of every object: half a bicycle, half a face, half the public fountain, whose water climbed upward and waited in a trembling column.

People tried to help. The baker offered flour, the mechanic offered oil, and the schoolchildren sang the alphabet backward. The yucca accepted each gift into its roots, yet tightened its leaves. By noon—which could not officially begin—the town had grown crowded with unfinished gestures. A handshake hovered between two palms. A sneeze became a bright glass bead in the air.

Mara, keeper of the bus timetable, noticed the wallaby staring into her emptied pouch. Inside was a small darkness shaped like a door. “Perhaps morning is not caught,” Mara said. “Perhaps it is afraid to leave.”

She knelt beside the yucca and told the darkness about ordinary things: spoons resting after breakfast, dust rehearsing its little dances, buses arriving three minutes late with warm seats. The wallaby listened. The yucca loosened one leaf. From the pouch came a faint rustle, as if a field were turning over in sleep.

Then morning crawled out by itself—not cloth now, but a barefoot child made of yellow paper. It climbed the yucca, opened every blossom, and leaped. The waiting water fell. The sneeze shattered into birdsong. Both halves of each face met and blinked.

Afterward, the wallaby refused the riverbed. She rode the number seven bus, sitting upright with her paws folded over the pouch. Mara altered the timetable: MORNING—WHEN READY. At the first yucca, the paper child still paused to whisper among the blades. No one knew what it said, but every window opened a little before the light arrived.
